# What I need: timesheets and overtime for Hollis Bakery

Hi, I'm Marisol Hollis. I own Hollis Bakery in Vancouver, Washington. We have 14 people: bakers who start late at night, counter staff in the daytime, two shift managers (Dee and Tom), and me. I'm not technical, so please bear with me.

## The problem

Everyone writes their hours on a paper sheet by the back door. On Sunday night I type the sheets into a spreadsheet, work out overtime by hand and send the totals to our payroll company. It takes me about two hours every week, and I still get it wrong: last quarter I underpaid overtime twice and had to fix it on the next paycheck. My accountant has warned me that getting overtime wrong can mean back pay and penalties. People also ask me "how many hours do I have this week?" and I have to go and find the paper.

## What I want to happen

- My people put in their own hours, and I stop typing anything.
- Overtime is always worked out right, so I can stop worrying about it.
- Payroll takes me 15 minutes, not two hours: I check the week, approve it and send one file.
- Anyone can see their own hours and pay for the week without asking me.

## Who uses it

- **Employees** put in their shifts (when they started and when they finished), look at their own week and send their timesheet in when the week is done.
- **Managers** (Dee, Tom and me) see the whole team's week, fix mistakes, approve timesheets or send them back, and make the file for payroll. Managers work shifts too, so they have their own timesheets.

## What I absolutely need

- Each person puts in their shifts for the week.
- A weekly timesheet for each person that they send in, and that a manager approves or sends back with a reason.
- Overtime worked out correctly: time and a half for the hours over 40 in a week, which is the federal rule. My accountant says Washington follows the same 40-hour rule for us, so that is all we need today. She sent me the Department of Labor's page on it: https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/overtime
- A way to fix a timesheet after it has been approved, because people forget things. I need to see afterwards who changed what, when, and why.
- A file for the payroll company (they take a CSV spreadsheet) with each person's regular and overtime hours and pay for the week. Only approved timesheets go in it.
- A page for managers that shows the whole team's week at a glance, like my spreadsheet: people down the side, days across the top, the totals and who has overtime.
- A simple page for employees with just their own week.
- Employees must not be able to see each other's hours or pay.
- Dee and Tom sometimes fix the same timesheet at the same moment, and in my spreadsheet one of them wipes out the other's fix. That must not happen.

## What would really help

- It works on a phone. Most of my people will use their phone, and the managers check the week on theirs.
- When someone leaves, I can take them off the list without losing their old timesheets.
- Mistakes are caught when they are typed, like a shift that finishes before it starts.

## Nice to have, if it's easy

- When I give someone a raise, I can say which week it starts from, and the weeks before it keep the old rate.
- A note on a shift ("covered for Sam").

## Not now

Scheduling or rotas, tips, taxes and deductions, paying people (the payroll company does that), a clock-in kiosk, and anything that needs an internet service. It should run on the computer in our back office.
